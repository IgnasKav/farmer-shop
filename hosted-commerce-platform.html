Hosted commerce.
Start with a paid pilot.

A Medusa-based platform with a Vue + Nuxt storefront for Lithuanian farmers selling directly to households. Farmers publish their produce, define delivery and collection schedules, and manage orders through a mobile-friendly store. Merchants pay monthly for their store, hosting, and maintenance.

2 developers 20-week working estimate Lithuanian farmers 3–5 pilot stores

Pilot: farm orders built around real delivery schedules

Recruit 3–5 farmers already taking direct orders through messages or phone calls. Start with Vilnius home deliveries and a few scheduled collection stops in Kaunas, including at least one farmer already using a chilled delivery provider.

Read the detailed farmer pilot plan → for delivery rules, farmer use cases, provider research, scope, and success criteria.

Run six live weeks to test whether farmers spend less time organising orders and whether customers can order without delivery clarification. Agree the pilot price and continuation price during recruitment.

Build only what the pilot needs

Core product

  • Lithuanian mobile storefront: products, fixed pack sizes, availability, cart, and guest checkout; one farmer per basket.
  • Medusa admin for products, inventory, orders, and refunds, extended with fulfilment schedules, packing totals, and orders grouped by date and stop.
  • Recurring home delivery, scheduled collection stops, farm collection, and provider delivery with cutoffs, capacity, fees, and date exceptions.
  • One payment provider with payment status, tax setup, order confirmations, and delivery or collection reminders.
  • Logo, colors, homepage content, and a custom domain per store.

Keep pilot operations manual

  • Set up each store and domain with a repeatable checklist.
  • Configure branding and import the initial catalog for merchants.
  • Collect the monthly platform fee through an existing billing service.
  • Handle onboarding and support directly with pilot merchants.
  • Book provider shipments manually and record tracking references; validate DPD Fresh terms before any integration.

Architecture: one shared codebase, with a separate Medusa deployment and database per merchant. Reuse the same Vue + Nuxt storefront template with store-specific settings.

Defer: a page builder, full custom merchant dashboard, automated provisioning, platform control system, plugin marketplace, native apps, shared baskets across farms, subscriptions, variable-weight settlement, automatic route optimisation, and multiple courier integrations. Focused farmer workflow extensions remain in scope.

Four implementation phases

Weeks Focus Done when
1–3 Define the pilot Niche and country are defined: Lithuanian farmers. Recruit 3–5 farms, document one real delivery week per farm, measure current admin time, agree pricing and payment provider, validate carrier terms, and approve the storefront workflow.
4–9 Build commerce A shopper can browse, select an eligible delivery or collection date, pay, and create a correct order in staging. Cutoffs, shared stock, capacity, and schedule exceptions work. Farmers can produce packing and stop lists.
10–14 Prepare operations Deploy stores, configure domains and branding, connect platform billing, and pass the launch checks below.
15–20 Run the paid pilot Run six live weeks with 3–5 farms. Target 100 completed orders, three farms active for four consecutive weeks, 30% less order-admin time, 90% of orders without fulfilment clarification, and three farmers agreeing to continue at a stated paid price. Track hosting and support costs.

Working estimate assumes two experienced developers full time: 14 weeks of preparation and six live pilot weeks, replacing the earlier 16-week outline. Scheduling adds implementation work; reassess after farm interviews and the first working checkout. Success targets are hypotheses, not forecasts.

Before taking live orders

  • Verify payments, webhook retries, inventory, shipping, taxes, order emails, and refunds.
  • Verify address eligibility, order cutoffs in Europe/Vilnius time, capacity limits, stock shared across delivery methods, skipped dates, and notifications for schedule changes.
  • Confirm product eligibility for each delivery method and carrier terms. Test shortages, agreed substitutions, cancellations, and refund status.
  • Check merchant access isolation; test a backup restore and deployment rollback.
  • Test browsing and checkout on real phones, including accessibility and loading speed.
  • Confirm the platform fee, failed-payment and cancellation handling, and data export process.
Expand scope after the paid pilot shows what merchants need and what it costs to serve them.
